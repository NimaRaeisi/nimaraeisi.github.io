About

Groundwater, permafrost and models

I am a PhD student in Geotechnical Engineering at the University of Calgary's Permafrost Engineering Laboratory, supervised by Dr. Jocelyn Hayley. I hold a BSc in Geology and an MSc in Hydrogeology from Kharazmi University of Tehran.

My doctoral research looks at how permafrost thaw changes groundwater–surface water interaction in discontinuous permafrost terrain, using the Giant Mine Remediation Project in the Northwest Territories as a case study. As a Mitacs-funded researcher working with WSP, I am building a coupled hydro-thermal model of the Baker Creek watershed.

Before coming to Calgary, I built MODFLOW groundwater models for Iran's Water Resources Company. For my MSc thesis I applied machine learning to forecast groundwater levels.

Research and projects

PhD research

Hydro-thermal modelling in discontinuous permafrost

Giant Mine Remediation Project, NT · May 2026 – present
  • Coupled hydro-thermal model of the Baker Creek watershed to study how thawing permafrost changes the exchange between groundwater and the creek.
  • Site characterization with ERT surveys, geotechnical testing and a conceptual site model.
  • Comparing Baker Creek diversion designs for their effect on thaw and groundwater flow.
  • Long-term climate scenarios of watershed response as permafrost degrades.
Remote sensing

Permafrost and water storage change in the Mackenzie River Basin

Independent project · 2026 – present
  • Python workflow that removes snow and soil moisture from GRACE/GRACE-FO data (2002–2026), producing 36 storage series for six subbasins.
  • Pre-whitened Mann–Kendall and Sen's slope trends, checked against HYDAT baseflow, precipitation and lake levels.
